# Redwoods: scene gate record

Not approved. Painted by `tools/scenes/redwoods.py`. The brief lists what failed before: trunks in a row (a barcode), a corridor with drooping boughs (eerie), round dabs (camouflage), sticks with needle lines (sickly pines).

## Tried and dropped, 16 November 2026

- A grove seen from outside, with wide domed crowns. The crowns read as domes or stupas, and the light between trunks read as windows.
- Small boughs growing from mid trunks. They read as fish bones, then as feathers.

## Current variants

All three are built on giant trunks of very different widths, unevenly spaced, cut off by the top of the card, pale ones stepping back into mist.

| Variant | What it is | Blind test |
|---|---|---|
| A, Two giants | A giant trunk at each side, pale trunks between, no foliage | "Misty forest interior", sure 4. The pale trunks read as columns, more so at Night. The bark streaks look slightly like a barcode |
| B, Among the trunks | One giant off centre, four more stepping back, one close branch with needles entering at top right | "Conifer forest", second guess "redwood or fir grove", sure 5. Told by the needled branch. The branch floats and could be a feather at a glance |
| C, Edge of the grove | One giant and two neighbours at left, open ground, a far forest wall, the same branch against the sky | "Forest edge with clearing", sure 4. The pale band under the far trees could be water, meadow or mist |

B passes the blind gate. Without the branch the trunks read as columns, so the branch is what names the place.

To do before the illustrator and the owner: attach the branch to a trunk or bring it in from the top edge so it does not float, and soften the bark streaks on the largest trunk.

## Owner, 16 November 2026: not accepted, first time

"None of the trees quite land for me. Redwoods have more foliage underneath like ferns, which are missing here. The perspectives and opacities are also misleading. Trees in the back can be lighter but not opaque." The owner sent four photographs of redwood groves as reference. They are reference only. Nothing is traced or copied from them.

What the photographs show that the paintings lacked:
- Ferns and low foliage across the whole floor, in front of the trunk bases.
- Sprays of foliage crossing in front of trunks at several depths, and hanging between them at the top.
- Bark as long fibrous furrows that follow the flare of the trunk.
- Trunks that get narrower and sit higher in the picture as they go back, in one consistent ground plane.
- Far trunks lighter in value but solid. Nothing shows through a nearer trunk.

Cause of the "misleading opacities": every wash was a transparent glaze, so far trunks and the floor showed through nearer trunks. Fix: each nearer shape is lifted back to paper before it is painted, so it covers what is behind it. Reading taken of "lighter but not opaque": far trees may be paler, but must not look see-through. This is a guess to confirm with the owner.

## Second version, 16 November 2026: one ground plane, solid trunks, ferns, foliage

Painted to the owner's notes. A tree's depth sets where its base sits, how wide it is and how dark it is. Each nearer shape covers what is behind it. Three variants: A two giants at the sides, B one giant right of centre, C a lower view with three large trunks.

Blind test: A "old conifer forest", second guess "redwood grove", sure 4. B the same, sure 4. C "big-trunk forest", second guess "stone columns in fog", sure 3. None passes the gate of 5.

What worked: the flared trunks named the place in A and B. No reader called a trunk see-through.

What failed:
- The sprays of foliage in front of the trunks. Read as fern or palm fronds hanging in mid-air, feathers, leaf prints, stains, and in C a fish skeleton or a bird. They looked see-through and attached to nothing.
- The ferns. Read as spiky grass tufts.

Next: remove every floating spray. Foliage is attached to something the eye can follow: a canopy mass at the top that the trunks pass into, or a small understory tree with its own stem. Ferns are fewer and larger, with clear arching fronds. Then the blind test again.
