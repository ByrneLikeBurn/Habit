# First run by the owner, 16 November 2026

The owner ran the signed Mac build of milestone 2 and gave feedback on the New habit form. This is the first feedback from a person using the app. Two review groups looked at the owner's screenshots: content designer, first-day user and neurodiversity; and UI and UX, human factors, senior designer and product owner. They disagreed on the Days control and both accepted a third option in a second round.

This file is separate from `docs/spec.md` while milestone 2 is being built. It moves there when that PR is merged.

## What the owner said

"Padding is an issue. More glyphs is preferable. Yes or no is too terse. The UI for choosing a day is unclear."

## Padding: a bug in the Mac build

The Mac sheet uses a form that puts labels in a left column. The name's example text is drawn outside its field, "Target each day" is drawn twice, content starts halfway across the sheet, fields touch the right edge, the day buttons wrap to two rows, and the Kind control hugs its text.

The Mac sheet is one column, 420 points wide, with 24 point margins. Labels sit above their fields. The example text sits inside the field. Sections are 20 points apart. The seven days stay on one row at 44 points each.

## Kind

"Yes or no" answers a question the form never asks, and "Kind" is abstract.

- Label: "Logging"
- Segments: "Mark done" and "Count". The segments fill the width.
- Under "Mark done": "One tap marks the day done."
- Under "Count": "Each tap adds to the day's total."

"Done or not" was ruled out because "or not" names the miss. "Mark done" is already the app's wording on the day sheet.

## Days

With all seven filled there was nothing to compare against, so filled did not read as on. The filled circles also matched the black Add button and read as seven main buttons.

- Heading: "Repeats on"
- The seven buttons are always visible, on one row, and stay out of "More".
- A chosen day has a heavy outline and a small checkmark above its letter, with no black fill, so it is lighter than Add. A day that is not chosen has a thin outline in secondary ink and the letter only. The checkmark and the stroke weight carry the state.
- Letters are two each at equal width ("Mo", "Tu"), in the order of the locale's week start.
- Two plain text buttons, "Set every day" and "Set weekdays", set the seven buttons. They have no selected look and 44 point targets. "Set weekdays" uses the locale's weekend.
- Summary line. All on: "Every day. Choose a day to leave it out." Some off: "Every day except Thursday". None on: "No days yet. Choose a day to add it."
- VoiceOver: "Monday, selected" and "Monday, not selected".
- The checkmark is used on this form only, never on Today or in History, where a filled mark means done.

Open, for the data lenses: whether Add stays on when no day is chosen. The neurodiversity lens wants it on. It is a data rule, because it allows a habit with no scheduled days.

## Symbols

- About 132 symbols, from Apple's own set.
- The form shows one row: "None", the six most common, and "More…".
- "More…" opens a grid that can be searched, in eleven groups of twelve: Body, Food and drink, Home, Making, Mind, Movement, Outdoors, People, Play, Rest, Work and study.
- The groups are in alphabetical order by the user's language. A hand-made order implies a rank, and the owner's point is that rest and play are as worth tracking as work.
- "Play" was added at the owner's request. The owner suggested "Fun and play". The reviews chose "Play": it is short enough for a chip and translates as a noun, and "Fun" and "Joy" name a feeling the user is then expected to have. Its symbols: game controller, dice, playing cards, puzzle piece, chess piece, ball, racket, kite, dancing figure, headphones, ticket, tent. Check them against the other groups for repeats.
- "People" and "Outdoors" were added when the owner asked what else was missing. People holds connection, family, community and pets, with a paw. The ceiling is twelve groups: two rows of six on the Mac, and on a phone a chip past the first four or five is found less often.
- Covered by an existing group, with the symbols it needs: health care tasks and hygiene in Body (pill, inhaler, appointment, talking); money in Home (coin, wallet, receipt); faith and reflection in Mind, with neutral symbols only (candle, open hands, sunrise), because a set of religious emblems always leaves someone out; time away from a screen in Rest, as a phone face down; learning in Work and study.
- Names left out because they read as a judgment or a demand: "Self-care", "Giving", "Screen limits".
- The six symbols on the form's first row include one for rest and one for play. Dice takes the place of the pencil or the leaf. With no analytics, "most common" is a guess.
- The default is no symbol. The build preselects the drop, and every input is optional.
- The owner asked for a filter by category. Under the search field is one row of chips: "All" and the eleven groups. It scrolls sideways on iPhone and wraps on the Mac. One category is on at a time, and "All" is the default, which shows the grid with its group headings. Typing in the search field searches every group and returns the chips to "All". The chosen chip has a heavy outline and a checkmark, like the day buttons. VoiceOver reads "Movement, selected, 7 of 12", then "12 symbols shown".

The product owner and senior designer wanted about 40 and no search. They accepted 96 because the one-row form is shorter than the form today.

## Also found

- "More" does not say what it holds. It reads "Unit and tap amount".
- History shows its title twice on the Mac, in the toolbar and as the heading.

## The owner's decisions

- Export with a password stays after the first version, as planned. The owner raised moving it forward and then said to hold it.
